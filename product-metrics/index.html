April 2026 — Overview

Reporting period: April 2026 · Released on: 15 May 2026

Initial Remarks

Budget comparisons this month should be read with caution: the 2026 budget was only finalised in mid-April, meaning the reference figures reflect a partial planning cycle. Sales performance was broadly positive across markets, driven mainly by the mix shift towards higher-TPV segments: Core Segments (Small + Medium) reached 48.4% of the total base, and the 2026 onboarding cohorts are already generating higher TPV per CID than the 2025 cohort average at equivalent maturity stages — a positive structural signal for revenue quality. Activation rates also improved, with D30 activation for the Mar-26 cohort reaching 64% — ahead of the D60 rate for the Feb-26 cohort — and the Apr-26 cohort's D15 rate already standing at 61%.

On the other hand, Take Rate closed 1bps below budget, though April showed early MoM recovery (+2bps). CR Tickets per CID fell from 0.49 to 0.41, driven in part by a reduction in blocked-funds contacts, the leading driver of inbound volume. Fraud Losses spiked to €487k (3.4bps of TPV); however, €100k remains under dispute and €135k has been flagged as a miscategorisation, placing the underlying net exposure materially below the headline figure.

Segment

  • Core Segments (Small + Medium) hit 48.4% of the active base (+3.5pp MoM) — on budget.
  • Nano net adds turned negative (−2,982), reflecting the deliberate segment mix intervention in effect.
  • Small added 3,858 net CIDs — the strongest individual contributor to the mix shift.
  • Large & Enterprise: 4 Large merchants stopped transacting in April - one churned merchant (Iceland) who notified us in Q2-25 but still had remaining trx volume in March; one bankruptcy (UK); one seasonal merchant (Hungary), and one administrative migration to a new Company ID (Iceland).

Product

  • Payments: TPV reached €1,414m — the highest month on record — +3% above budget and +6.5% MoM. DCC closed 2% below budget, although it started to recover with the increasing number of non-domestic transactions.
  • Business Accounts: 12.7k active (+11.3% MoM); Card Spend €19.6m (+70% vs budget); Total Banking Revenue €289k (+45% vs budget).
  • 3P Credit: 3P Loans Issue reached 7.7m generating €424k in Commission Revenue; 3P Credit launched in HU and HR, with first 13 deals totalling €166k.
  • Customer Support Platform: Together with the CR Ticket per CID drop to 0.41, AI automation rate reached 8.44% (highest level recorded) with CSAT on automated tickets at 87.2%.

Country

  • UK: largest market at 31.6k CIDs (+5.1% MoM); Gross Adds +4% against budget.
  • Croatia: peak season has begun, with re-onboards and reactivation resulting in 19.3% MoM increase in Active CIDs; card spend surged over 5x MoM as onboardings accelerated.
  • Portugal has improved activation rates reaching +60% in D15, the best performance seen in record.

Trends to Watch — May 2026

  • Activation Rate & Registration Flow rollout — Early cohort data is encouraging (Mar-26 D30: 64%; Apr-26 D15: 61%). The full rollout of the new Registration flow in the UK in May will be a key leading indicator of structural activation improvement across the onboarding base.
  • Sales performance & Summer Season — The approaching summer peak presents an opportunity to accelerate volume; the evolution of segment mix will be closely monitored as onboarding activity grows.
  • Business Account Deposits — BA adoption is expected to increase in the EUR countries, with higher Deposit Balance per active account.
  • Fraud Loss control — The April spike to €487k (3.4bps) is partly explained by disputed and miscategorised items. Disputes are running and might lead to recovery this month. With volumes growing into the summer season, maintaining fraud losses within budget tolerance is a priority.

Commentary

The active base grew to 73.3k CIDs in April driven by a Gross Adds performance +10% above budget. Core Segments (Small + Medium) reached 48.4% of the total base, up 3.5pp MoM and at the target level for the month. Croatia was the fastest-growing market at +19.3% MoM.   • Stonecrest Marble (UK) went bankrupt and was terminated at the end of the month.
  • Trade Fairs (Hungary) is a seasonal merchant who uses Teya in events and hosted a big one in March. They have not been terminated and another event is expected to take place in Q2-26.
  • Fjallakofinn (Iceland) is a safety equipment seller who churned to another acquirer. Customer Operations is maintaining close communication with the merchant.
  • A second Icelandic merchant does not constitute an actual churn, as it has recently changed its SSN, leading to the creation of a new Company ID in Teya's systems.

Commentary

Active Business Accounts grew MoM to 12.7k but remain (4%) below budget, with overall penetration over total Active Base of countries where BA is live at 30%. March and April cohorts achieved >42% penetration, still not at the desired level. On the other hand, existing users are showing good signs of adoption: Deposit Balance reached €41.1m (+13.5% MoM), +1% above budget, driven by an increase in UK merchants in the >€10k deposit bucket (~€3.2m MoM uplift) and higher-balance merchants deepening their use of BA as a primary account; Card Spend also came in above expectations, with Croatia card spend surging over 5x MoM and card users nearly tripling as onboardings outpaced March's total by mid-month. The higher engagement with Teya Card resulted in an Interchange Revenue of €222k, +73% above budget, while the Average Daily Deposit Balance for the month stood at €38.8m, (5%) below budget — the reference base for Deposit Interest Revenue, which came in 6% below plan.

3P Loans Issued was €7.7m (−1% vs budget) with 309 deals completed. We launched 3P Credit in HU and HR in April, with 13 deals funded to date totalling €166k (€12k average) - below our expectations. Finmid made fewer offers available than expected in both markets — per Finmid, our merchants' seasonality was not accurately modelled in the original business case. We are working with them to increase offer availability. April was also shortened by bank holidays, which compressed conversion broadly.

Commentary

Customer Support volume continued to decline in April, reaching 0.41 CR Tickets per CID, beating target by 8%. On a 30-day rolling basis, Tickets per CID have been consistently dropping week-over-week, reaching 0.39 in the last week of April. Reductions were observed across most geographies, with the exception of Croatia, where an uptick in ticket volume is expected with the start of summer season.

Action items defined under IMO#4 are generating tangible results, reducing ticket volume related to blocked funds — the most pressing contact reason — by 24%. AI CSAT has improved to 87%, highest record.

On the experience side, overall NPS held steady at 65 in April, unchanged from March. Platform reliability recovered fully to 99.95% following the March dip to 99.87%, returning within target tolerance.

Initial Remarks

The booked figure of €487k (3.4 bps of TPV) is materially above the April target of €294k. €75k booked against Italy has already been returned (1st May) and €80k relates to an insolvency mis-categorised as fraud (Stonecrest Marble, now reclassified as a credit loss). On a like-for-like basis, the adjusted fraud loss is closer to €335k — with a further €100k still under dispute — and the three-month rolling average remains below budget at €270k versus a target of €328k.

The month was driven by a small number of larger cases. The Italy exposure (€107k booked) stems from an issuer-side card compromise involving 61 chargebacks, 49 from a single issuer (Transact Payments Malta Ltd); the full €268k portfolio is under dispute and recovery prospects are considered strong. The Regal Rose / Imperial Clothing case (UK) reflects a pre-blocked-parties onboarding gap rather than active fraud, with £41k in dispute. The long tail — 16 cases below €1k and 24 between €1k–€10k — demonstrates that transaction monitoring controls are containing individual loss exposure. The focus for May is enhanced onboarding-level detection through new screening vendor partnerships.
